Binding Methods ▾
Handwork / Collating Manual assembly of printed sections in the correct order before binding. Typically charged per copy.
Manual collation & assembly
Guillotine (Post-binding) Final trimming of bound books to achieve a clean, even edge. Three-knife trimming is typical for perfect bound books.
Final trim to finished size
Saddle Stitch Wire staples are driven through the folded spine of a booklet. Best for booklets up to 72 pages. Economical and fast. Cannot be used for thick books.
Wire stitch through folded spine
Perfect Binding Pages are glued to a square spine using hot-melt EVA adhesive. Used for books 60+ pages. Gives a professional square-spine finish like a paperback novel.
Glued square spine (EVA)
Wire-O / Coil Bind A metal or plastic coil or double-loop wire is threaded through punched holes. Allows the book to lie flat when open. Used for calendars, reports, manuals.
Double-loop wire or spiral coil
Case Binding (Hardcover) The most durable binding: sewn signatures glued into a rigid board cover with book cloth or paper. Used for premium reports, commemorative books, diaries. Most expensive binding option.
Sewn & hardboard cover (premium)
Stab Stitch Wire stitches are driven through the side (not the spine) of the stacked pages. Used for exercise books, notebooks, and thick booklets where saddle stitching is not possible.
Side-stitch through full thickness
Loop Stitch Like saddle stitch but the wire forms a loop on the spine, allowing the document to be inserted into a ring binder. Common for reports and catalogues sent to binder-filing clients.
Ring-binder compatible spine loops
Thermal Binding Pages are inserted into a pre-glued cover spine that activates with heat. Quick and clean for short-run reports (5–100 copies). Less durable than perfect binding for heavy use.
Heat-activated glue spine
Coptic / Smyth Sewn Signatures are sewn together with thread before casing in. Highest durability; the book opens completely flat. Used for premium bibles, journals, and archival publications.
Thread-sewn signatures (premium)
Lamination A plastic film bonded to the cover (or all pages). Gloss = shiny, vivid. Matt = soft, premium look. Protects against moisture and wear. Cover lamination only is most common.
Plastic film coat on cover

Finishing & Special Processes ▾
Add only the processes that apply to this job. Leave blank if not applicable.
Perforation A line of small holes punched through the paper so a section can be torn off cleanly. Used for tickets, receipts, reply cards, and vouchers.
Tear-off perf line (inline or offline)
Padding Notepads and message pads are made by stacking sheets, applying padding compound (a flexible glue) along one edge, then backing with chipboard. Charged per pad.
Notepad gluing + chipboard back
Scoring / Creasing A crease is pressed into the paper to allow it to fold cleanly without cracking the ink. Essential for heavy card covers and brochures. Prevents cracking on fold lines.
Fold line impression on card/board
Machine Folding Automated folding of printed sheets. Types include: half-fold, roll fold (4-panel), Z-fold (concertina), gate fold. Much faster and more accurate than hand-folding.
MBO or similar: roll, Z, gate fold
Machine Numbering Sequential numbers printed on each copy by a numbering head. Used for tickets, receipts, certificates, and any document requiring a unique reference number.
Sequential numbering unit
Hole Drilling / Punching Holes drilled or punched through the finished document for ring binders or filing. Standard 2-hole or 4-hole patterns. Charged per thousand sheets.
Ring-binder or filing holes
Foil Stamping A metallic or coloured foil is pressed onto the paper using a heated die. Gives a premium look to covers, certificates, and invitations. Gold, silver, and holographic foils are most common.
Hot foil blocking (gold, silver, custom)
Embossing / Debossing Embossing raises the paper surface to create a 3D raised image (emboss) or pressed-in image (deboss). Often combined with foil on premium covers. Requires a custom die.
Raised or recessed impression
Die Cutting A custom steel blade die is pressed into the paper to cut a specific shape — window cutouts, custom package shapes, unusual book shapes. The die itself is a one-time setup cost.
Custom shape / window cutting
Spot UV A UV-cured glossy coating applied to specific areas of a page (e.g. a logo or image) for contrast and premium effect. The rest of the page may be matt. Requires a film to define the spot area.
Selective UV gloss coat
Overall Varnish A clear varnish applied over the entire printed surface. Gloss varnish intensifies colours; matt varnish reduces glare. Less expensive than lamination but less durable.
Flood or selective varnish coat
Shrink Wrapping Individual copies or bundles are wrapped in clear plastic film then shrunk tight with a heat gun or tunnel. Protects during delivery and gives a retail-ready appearance.
Individual or bundle shrink wrap
Eyeletting Metal eyelets inserted into holes for strength and aesthetics. Used for certificates, tags, and banners where a cord or string must pass through.
Metal ring inserts in punched holes
Index Tab Cutting Tabs cut from the page edge to create visible dividers in directories, bibles, and reference books. Requires a special die or tab-cutting machine.
Thumb-index tabs for dividers
Collating / Machine Sort Automated gathering of printed sections into the correct page order using a collating machine. Faster and more accurate than hand-collating for large runs.
Automated page-order assembly
